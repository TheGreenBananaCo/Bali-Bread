Why green banana flour?

First things first: it doesn’t taste like banana.

Green banana flour is mild, versatile and naturally rich in resistant starch. In Bali Bread, it creates something entirely different from the sweet banana flavor people expect from ripe fruit. This isn’t banana bread. It’s bread made differently.

Green bananas have a very different nutritional profile from ripe bananas. Most importantly for us, they’re naturally rich in resistant starch — a type of starch that resists digestion in the small intestine and reaches the colon, where it can be fermented by gut bacteria. That difference is a big part of why we started working with the flour in the first place.

We first encountered green banana flour years ago in Bali. We were surprised by how well it worked in food and came back fascinated by an ingredient we’d barely heard of in the United States. That curiosity eventually became Bali Bread.

  • Feeds your gut bacteria. Resistant starch passes through the small intestine undigested, and in the colon your gut bacteria ferment it into short-chain fatty acids, which help nourish the cells lining the gut.
  • Starch that digests slowly. Resistant starch is not broken down into sugar in the small intestine the way ordinary starch is.
  • Simple ingredients, minimally processed. Nothing fortified, nothing artificial.

The Science

What’s actually in the flour

These are the numbers for green banana flour itself, measured per 100g of flour. We’ve put them here rather than on the bread, because that’s where they honestly belong — see the note underneath.

40g

Resistant starch / 100g raw green banana flour

A fermentable fiber, and by far the highest concentration in anything we sell. Heat is what destroys it, so the flour is where it survives: stir it into a smoothie or overnight oats and it arrives the way it left the mill.

More resistant starch

Even after baking

Baking reduces resistant starch, but research shows that bread made with green banana flour can retain higher levels than conventional bread. So while the raw flour contains far more, the benefit doesn’t simply disappear when it goes into the oven.

1,491mg

Potassium / 100g raw green banana flour

Plus magnesium, phosphorus, and antioxidant polyphenols — naturally, with no fortification.

4

Flours in the loaf

Green banana, rice, tapioca and corn make up our flour blend. No wheat, rye or barley ingredients.

A note about baking: heat reduces the resistant starch naturally present in green banana flour, although some remains and some can reform as the loaf cools. The raw flour therefore contains substantially more resistant starch than the finished bread.

Sources: CIQUAL/CNEVA (1993); Bezerra et al., 2013; Journal of Nutritional Science, 2022; Plants 14(2):207, 2025.

Frequently asked questions

Does it taste like banana?

No. Green banana flour doesn’t have the sweetness or familiar banana flavor of ripe fruit. Bali Bread tastes like bread — not banana bread.

Is there gluten in it?

Bali Bread contains no wheat, rye or barley ingredients. Our current Santa Barbara kitchen is not a dedicated certified gluten-free facility, so we cannot guarantee against cross-contact. If you have celiac disease or a serious allergy, please contact us before ordering.

What about other allergens?

Our pancake & waffle mix and quick-bread premix call for you to add one egg at home — the bag itself has no egg in it. Everything we sell is baked fresh here in Santa Barbara under California’s Cottage Food Operation law, not in a certified allergen-controlled facility, so we can’t guarantee any product is free of cross-contact with other allergens. If you have a serious allergy, message us before you order and we’ll tell you exactly what’s been in the kitchen.

Can I pause or cancel my subscription?

Yes. Pause, skip a delivery, switch plans or cancel from the link in your confirmation email, or just reply to it and we will do it for you.

Do you deliver outside Isla Vista, Goleta, and Santa Barbara?

Not yet. We're starting hyper-local so every loaf is fresh. Sign up for bake alerts and tell us your area — expansion plans will follow demand.

Why only two bakes a week?

Because fresh bread is better bread. We’re starting small, baking to demand and keeping the time between oven and customer as short as possible. Sunday and Wednesday keep the bread fresh without baking more than Santa Barbara actually wants.

How should I store it?

Keep it on the counter in its bag for up to two days. After that, slice and freeze it, then toast straight from frozen. Skip the refrigerator — it makes bread stale faster.
